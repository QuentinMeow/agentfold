# Are the questions this repository puts in front of you now readable enough to answer on their own?

**Action:** After the repair is published as one exact revision, judge whether every human-attention file is understandable and answerable on its own, and approve it, name the remaining ambiguity, or reject the format.
**Why this matters:** This shape governs every question an agent will ever ask you, so if it is hard to read, every future request is hard to read.
**If you do nothing:** Nothing stops; the rewritten shape becomes and stays the standard, and your judgment on it is never recorded.

## What you need to know

**Today:** you already judged the underlying arrangement — you accepted what it does and said the way it read was wrong. The rewrite is in progress and has not been published as one finished version, so there is nothing to judge here yet. This file exists so the promise to bring it back to you cannot quietly disappear meanwhile.

**What this would change:** once the rewrite is finished, you would say whether a stranger could answer each of these files correctly from the file alone, without wanting to ask a question first. Approving makes that shape the standard for every question an agent asks you afterwards.

**What this does not decide:** how often you are asked anything, or which questions reach you. That is set elsewhere and does not move either way.

The standard aimed at is concrete. A question should open by saying what it wants and what happens if you ignore it, say plainly what happens today — including "nothing is built yet" when that is true — lay out each answer with its cost and one example of life afterwards, recommend one and argue against itself, and let you reply in one ordinary sentence with no vocabulary to copy. Everything a machine needs sits below your answer. The arrangement being rewritten is described in [the queue contract](../../../message-queue/AGENTS.md).

## Your choices

The choices differ on whether the rewritten shape becomes the standard as published, goes back for one named repair first, or is abandoned.

### Approve
Every question you receive from then on uses this shape, and later work builds on it instead of reopening it. The cost is a fairly long format: even a small question carries a "what happens today", a set of choices, and a recommendation.
*Example consequence:* a one-line yes/no question arrives as a page you read in forty seconds on your phone — better than a paragraph of jargon, longer than "yes or no?".

### Request changes
Something is still unclear. Name it; an agent repairs it and brings the changed version back. The cost is that the questions already in your queue keep their current shape until it lands.
*Example consequence:* you say the recommendation should come first because you usually just take it, and every future question is reordered around that.

### Reject
The shape is wrong and the rewrite starts from a different premise, losing the work behind it and leaving today's format in place meanwhile.
*Example consequence:* questions go back to being written however each agent feels like writing them, and you go back to opening other files to work out what you are agreeing to.
