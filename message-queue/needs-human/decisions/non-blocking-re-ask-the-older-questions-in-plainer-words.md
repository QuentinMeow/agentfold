# Should the nine older questions waiting on you be re-asked in plainer words?

**Action:** Choose whether the nine older waiting questions are left as written, replaced with plainer versions, or replaced only where their wording is now stale.
**Why this matters:** Nine of the questions in your queue were written in a format that buries the choice under bookkeeping, and four of them still ask about a moment that has already passed.
**If you do nothing:** They stay exactly as written and stay answerable; only questions filed from now on use the readable shape.

## What you need to know

**Today:** nine questions are waiting on you in an older layout. Each opens with roughly ten lines of machine bookkeeping before it says what is being asked, several bury the actual choice under a heading called "Differences", and five of them ended by telling you to copy a long hash into a field — an instruction that has been wrong since agents took that field over.

**What this would change:** whether those nine are withdrawn and replaced with versions that lead with the question, or left alone. Plainer versions of all nine are already drafted and readable in `history/conversations/2026-08-01-2317PDT-make-agent-reports-readable/artifacts/proposed-queue-rewrites/`.

**What this does not decide:** the questions themselves. Every draft keeps the same choice, the same consequences, and the same answer slot; what changes is the order and the words.

An agent cannot make this change alone, deliberately. A live question's visible text — title, context, choices — is its identity, and the repository refuses to let an agent reword one while it waits on you, because no check can tell "clearer" from "different". The rewrite was attempted and refused; that refusal is the system working. The rule is in [the guide to asking a human for something](../../../handbook/human-action-guide.md).

Four of the nine are stale in a way plainer wording will not fix: they ask you to approve something "before merge", and those merges already happened.

## Your choices

The choices differ in how many of the nine are withdrawn and re-asked.

### Option A — Leave all nine as written
Nothing changes. The drafts stay as a record of what they could have said.
*Example consequence:* you open one on your phone, scroll past ten lines of hashes and paths to find the question, and answer it anyway — or put it off again, which is what has happened so far.

### Option B — Replace all nine
Each is withdrawn with a recorded note saying it was replaced for readability, and a successor is filed carrying the same choice in plainer words.
*Example consequence:* your queue reads as nine questions instead of nine records, and the audit trail gains nine short disposition notes explaining why each original was retired unanswered.

### Option C — Replace only the four whose wording is stale
The four that ask about a merge that already happened are withdrawn and re-asked about the code as it exists now. The other five stay as written.
*Example consequence:* the four questions you could not have answered correctly become answerable, and the five that were merely dense stay dense.

## What I recommend

**Recommendation:** Option C — the four stale ones are the only ones where the current wording could lead you to a wrong answer, and withdrawing a question purely for style spends audit trail on presentation.
**Strongest case against this:** if the reason these nine have gone unanswered is that they are unpleasant to read, then C fixes four of them and leaves the actual cause in place for the other five.
**Confidence:** medium — I read all nine and drafted all nine replacements, so I know what each says; I do not know why they have gone unanswered, and that is the fact that would decide between B and C.
